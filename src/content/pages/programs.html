01 · Montessori

Order, independence and real materials.

Developed by Dr. Maria Montessori in 1907, the approach treats the educator as a guide. Children choose purposeful work and master it at their own pace, in a room where everything is child-sized and within reach.

Practical life

Pouring, dressing, setting the table, caring for plants and each other. The everyday skills that build confidence and concentration.

The senses

Materials that sort by texture, shape, size, colour, weight and temperature, laying quiet foundations for later math.

Language

Vocabulary, sounds and letter shapes, then the wooden movable alphabet, writing and, in time, reading.

Mathematics

One-to-one counting, the decimal system, then early operations, all with self-correcting hands-on materials.

Geography and culture

The earth, space, countries and traditions. Families are invited to share their own cultures with the class.

Science

Botany, zoology and simple physics. Questions, experiments and a great deal of wonder.

02 · Reggio-inspired

A hundred languages of learning.

Children are capable, curious people with ideas worth taking seriously. Art, music, dance, drama, design and storytelling become the ways they explore and explain them.

  • Projects that follow curiosity. Long-term investigations grow from the children's own questions.
  • Learning made visible. Educators document thinking through photos, video and the children's own words.
  • Shared with you daily. That documentation reaches parents throughout the day through brightwheel.

Our philosophy

“

Joy, feeling one's own value, being appreciated and loved by others, feeling useful and capable of production are all factors of enormous value for the human soul.

Dr. Maria Montessori

We see every child as competent, with their own abilities, interests and values. Our aim is to develop the whole child: independence, a positive sense of self, creativity, critical thinking and the ability to solve problems.
